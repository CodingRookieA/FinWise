How to Start Investing

Investing is the act of putting money to work with the goal of earning a return. It's a way to potentially grow your wealth over time.

Why should you invest?

Investing helps your money grow faster than it would in a regular savings account. While savings accounts offer stability, they typically provide minimal returns that may not keep pace with inflation. Investing in assets like stocks, bonds, or mutual funds can offer higher potential returns over the long term.

Getting started with investing

Before you begin investing, assess your financial situation. Make sure you have an emergency fund covering 3-6 months of expenses and that high-interest debt is under control. Once you're ready, determine your investment goals and time horizon.

Types of investments

Stocks represent ownership in a company. When you buy stocks, you become a shareholder and can benefit from the company's growth through price appreciation and dividends.

Bonds are loans you make to governments or corporations. In return, they pay you interest over time and return your principal when the bond matures.

Mutual funds and ETFs pool money from many investors to buy a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets. They offer instant diversification and professional management.

Risk and return

Generally, investments with higher potential returns come with higher risk. Stocks can be volatile but historically offer strong long-term returns. Bonds are more stable but typically provide lower returns. Diversification across different asset types helps manage risk.

Starting small

You don't need a lot of money to start investing. Many platforms allow you to begin with as little as $50 or $100. The key is to start early and invest consistently, taking advantage of compound returns over time.
